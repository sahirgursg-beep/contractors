Arizona contractor license requirements

Yes. The Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC) licenses commercial, residential and dual contractors statewide. A license is needed for jobs over $1,000 and for any job that needs a permit.

When you need a license

Under Arizona's handyman exemption, you can work without a license only when labor and materials total $1,000 or less and the job does not need a building permit. If a permit is required, you need a license whatever the price. Bills to raise the limit have been filed in recent sessions, but none has become law.

Commercial, residential or dual

  • Commercial: A (general engineering), B-1 and B-2 (general commercial) and the C specialty classes.
  • Residential: B (general residential), B-3 to B-10 and the R specialty classes.
  • Dual: KA, KB-1, KB-2 and the CR classes, which cover both residential and commercial work.

How to get a license

  1. Name a qualifying party. The qualifying party must be regularly employed by the business and have the experience for the classification.
  2. Pass the exams. The qualifying party passes the Statutes and Rules exam and the trade exam, unless they qualify for an in-state or out-of-state waiver. For B-1, B-2, KB-1 and KB-2, the NASCLA commercial building exam can replace the ROC trade exam.
  3. Background check, ID and bond. Everyone listed on the application submits a background check and ID, and the bond goes in with a bond verification form.
  4. Apply and pay. Residential and dual applicants also pay into the Residential Contractors' Recovery Fund.

License fees cover two years. Residential and dual renewals carry a $270 recovery fund assessment.

Bonds

The bond depends on the license scope and the yearly volume you expect. A residential general contractor expecting less than $750,000 a year posts a $9,000 bond; $750,000 or more posts $15,000. Commercial bonds go up to $50,000 for more than $10 million a year. A dual license adds the residential and commercial amounts together.

Instead of paying the recovery fund assessment, a residential contractor can post a $200,000 bond. The fund can repay a homeowner harmed by a licensed residential contractor up to $30,000.
